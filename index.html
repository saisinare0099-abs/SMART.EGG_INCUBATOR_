01 / Why

A controlled environment.
A practical learning project.

The report calls this project the Automatic Egg Incubator. The team confirms an Arduino Nano controller, a 20-egg tray capacity and manual egg turning. The documented automation concerns temperature-based heater switching, not fully automatic incubation.

01 — THE NEED

Consistent attention

Incubation depends on appropriate temperature, humidity, ventilation and egg handling. Manual monitoring needs frequent attention; this project investigates a simple automated alternative.

02 — THE APPROACH

Sense, decide, act

Read temperature and humidity, compare temperature with two switching thresholds, drive the heater relay, and show the readings and heater state on an LCD.

03 — THE BOUNDARY

Evidence before claims

Source-code behavior is documented here. Thermal stability, power consumption and hatch performance still need measured evidence. A control demo is not a successful hatch trial.

Our question: can an accessible sensor-and-controller system maintain suitable chamber conditions, once its hardware and performance are tested?

Reported practice and reference guidance

Team-reported routine: the eggs are chicken eggs, with manual turning described as twice over three days. The trial is still in progress, with two days elapsed at the latest team update. This is a stated routine, not a completed three-day observation log or a recommended incubation schedule. Exact turning timestamps have not been supplied; evenly spaced turns are not assumed.

Reference guidance: University of Missouri Extension recommends turning at least three times daily during the turning phase. Mississippi State University Extension advises stopping turning during the final three days before the expected hatch.

Review needed: twice over three days is below that daily-turning guidance for the normal turning phase. Review the routine with the project guide for the current and any future trial. The reported routine is not presented as a validated method or evidence of hatch success.

At or below this reading, request heater ON. This is the ON threshold from the supplied code.

At or above this reading, request heater OFF and buzzer ON. The alarm shares the heater-OFF threshold; it is not a separate high-temperature margin.

Request heater OFF and display an error. In the cleaned reference sketch, noTone() explicitly stops any previous buzzer tone.

Settings are not results. These values reproduce the submitted code; they are not a species-specific incubation recommendation, evidence of temperature stability, or an independent safety limit. Confirm the intended incubation schedule and validate the hardware before use.

Safety comes before long-duration trials. The supplied build photo shows corrugated-board enclosure surfaces near the intended heated chamber. It does not establish a safe finished heater installation. Some component photos also show exposed connection ends and taped mounting; their current energized state is not established. A qualified supervisor should review insulated connections, secure mounting, heat shielding, non-combustible clearances, independent over-temperature protection, fusing and moisture separation. Do not handle exposed connections while energized. Do not treat the software relay-OFF request as fail-safe protection.
